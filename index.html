Where money goes matters as much as whether it's given.

How to give well

  • Cash beats goods. Shipping, sorting and customs waste money and can clog relief routes.
  • Favor unrestricted funds. Responders can send money where the need is greatest.
  • Monthly beats one-off. Appeals spike after a disaster, but recovery takes years.
  • Give to forgotten crises. See which appeals are underfunded on UN OCHA FTS.
  • Local vs international. Local groups are faster and cheaper but harder to vet. Pooled funds such as IFRC DREF and UN CERF are a middle path.
  • Tax. Deductibility depends on your country (e.g. 80G in India). Check locally.

Verify before you give

Scam red flags

  • Pressure to give right now
  • Gift cards, crypto or wire transfers only
  • Names that mimic well-known charities
  • Unsolicited messages after a disaster

Other ways to help

  • Donate blood through official channels.
  • Volunteer with established organizations before a disaster; untrained arrivals can get in the way.
  • Share only verified information, with its source.
  • Check on neighbors, especially elderly people and those living alone.
